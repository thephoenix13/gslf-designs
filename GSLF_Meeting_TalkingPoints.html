Situation Brief

Who is GSLF & What Do They Need?

GSLF (Gram Swaraj Laghu Finance / skill-sector NGO) runs 400+ vocational skill centres across 12+ states, has trained 20,000+ students and placed 16,000+. Their current website is a 2019-era brochure with Lorem Ipsum still live. The BRD — commissioned after a competitive review of Pratham.org and QuestAlliance.net — calls for a full redesign that repositions GSLF as a Skill-to-Employment Platform, not just an NGO. They have a for-profit arm FGSPL that must be co-represented carefully.

Strategic Context

Why This Project Matters to GSLF

  • 1
    The website is their biggest lead funnel — currently broken Active centres in Aurangabad, Hyderabad, and Jammu are missing. Copyright is 2023. Fake 2019 blog posts are live. Students searching for courses can't find or trust them.
  • 2
    They benchmarked Pratham and Quest Alliance — and want to beat both GSLF has something neither competitor offers: salary-outcome data and an employer portal. The BRD is explicit — lead with employment outcomes, not mission text.
  • 3
    Three distinct revenue/impact streams need separate journeys Student enrolments, Employer hiring, Donor/CSR funding — all on one domain. The homepage must answer all three within 5 seconds. Current site answers none.
  • 4
    They have a for-profit entity (FGSPL) that needs careful co-branding Legal and branding disclosure is a BRD requirement. FGSPL is the employer-facing / apprenticeship arm. Mishandling this creates legal and credibility risk.
Talking Points — Opening (First 5 Minutes)

How to Open the Meeting

  • 1
    "We've gone through your BRD in detail — and we have a specific observation." Signal you're not just another vendor. You've done the homework. This earns attention immediately.
  • 2
    "GSLF's placement rate of 80%+ is your single biggest differentiator — and it's completely invisible on your current site." 16,000 placed out of 20,000 trained = 80% placement. Neither Pratham nor Quest can say this. Open with impact, not your portfolio.
  • 3
    "The BRD is ambitious. Our job as your product partner is to protect Phase 1 from scope creep." Positions you as a strategic co-pilot, not an order-taker. The BRD has 3 phases — keep the focus on launch essentials.
Talking Points — Product & Delivery

What You Bring to the Table

  • 1
    Audience-first architecture — not page-first We design the navigation and journey for 4 distinct audiences (Student, Employer, Donor, Partner) — each landing on a path that answers their primary question within seconds, not scrolling.
  • 2
    Editable everything — no vendor lock-in The BRD mandates editable impact counters, course details, job openings, and ticker. We build on a CMS so your team updates everything without a developer call.
  • 3
    Zoho CRM integration from Day 1 Every lead form — student, employer, donor, CSR — flows directly into Zoho. No manual follow-up gaps. KPIs are trackable from launch.
  • 4
    AI Chatbot that qualifies, not just greets Phase 2 Not a generic "How can I help?" — a structured course-finder: Education level → Location → Interest → Job goal → Recommended course + nearest centre + WhatsApp handoff. Zoho CRM integration also Phase 2. Phase 1 includes the chatbot UI wireframe and flow design only, so Phase 2 build is fast.
  • 5
    FGSPL co-branding handled correctly Footer disclosures, cross-linking, consistent UX between GSLF and FGSPL — structured to avoid legal ambiguity while reinforcing the shared mission story for donors.
Your Positioning in This Meeting

Do This / Don't Do That

✅ DO

  • Lead with their 80% placement stat — make them feel proud of what they have
  • Use the BRD's own language back to them ("Skill-to-Employment Platform")
  • Reference Pratham & Quest Alliance — they benchmarked these, it shows you read the BRD
  • Focus on Phase 1 clarity — what launches, what waits
  • Ask about content readiness early — this is their #1 risk
  • Mention KPI tracking from Day 1 — the BRD has 10 explicit KPIs

🚫 DON'T

  • Don't pitch your portfolio before understanding their urgency
  • Don't use "platform" language on donor pages — the BRD explicitly says soften it there
  • Don't promise go-live dates before content ownership is confirmed
  • Don't bundle Phase 2/3 features into Phase 1 pricing — scope will balloon
  • Don't ignore the FGSPL question — it will surface later if you don't address it
Discovery Questions to Ask in the Meeting

Your 12 Must-Ask Questions

  • 📅
    Q1. What is the real deadline driving this? Is there a funder review, government audit, or board presentation? The BRD says Go-Live is TBD — find the real urgency behind this project.
  • 📝
    Q2. Content readiness — where are you today on the 10+ content items listed in Section 10? Success stories, financials, team photos, course data — the BRD notes the 2019 Lorem Ipsum disaster. Who owns content delivery now?
  • 🔧
    Q3. Is the existing WordPress installation being kept, or are you open to replacing it? BRD says vendor should recommend. Your answer here positions your tech stack choice.
  • 🏢
    Q4. What is the FGSPL website situation today — does it exist, who manages it, what's the relationship with this redesign? The BRD requires UX consistency between both sites and cross-linking. You need to scope this.
  • 🤖
    Q5. On the AI Chatbot — do you have a Zoho account active? Who is the internal tech owner for the CRM integration? Chatbot UI design is Phase 1; full chatbot build + Zoho integration moves to Phase 2. Confirm they're aligned on this phasing early.
  • 🗺️
    Q6. For the interactive India map — do you have a Google Maps API key, and is the centre location data clean and ready? This is a Phase 1 must-have in the BRD. If data isn't ready, it becomes a blocker.
  • 📊
    Q7. The impact dashboard must update every 15 days — who internally owns that data and how is it currently tracked? If it's manual spreadsheets, you need to design the update workflow into the CMS, not just the front-end.
  • 💬
    Q8. WhatsApp Business API — is your account set up? Do you have a verified number? Sticky WhatsApp button and counsellor CTA are Must Haves in the BRD. Without the API account, you can't build the handoff.
  • 🌐
    Q9. Hindi language toggle — how much content do you expect to translate, and who does the Hindi copywriting? The BRD mandates Hindi on student and course pages. If GSLF isn't providing translations, it must be scoped.
  • 💰
    Q10. Do you have a budget range in mind, and is this a fixed-price engagement or milestone-based? Ask diplomatically after you've established value. The BRD has a 3-phase scope — phased billing might appeal to them.
  • 🧑‍💼
    Q11. Who is the single decision-maker — is it Devjit (CTO), the CEO, or a committee? What does approval look like? The BRD approval table lists CEO + COO + CTO. Know who you need to convince and what the final sign-off process is.
  • 🔁
    Q12. Post-launch support — what's your expectation? Monthly retainer, ad-hoc, or handover to internal team? The BRD lists post-launch support as 10% of vendor evaluation weight. Clarify upfront to avoid ambiguity.
Risks You Should Raise Proactively

Red Flags in the BRD

⚠️ Content Black Hole

10+ content types are GSLF's responsibility — success stories, financials, photos, batch schedules. No content = no go-live. Ask for a content readiness date before signing.

⚠️ No Go-Live Date Set

TBD go-live is a red flag. Without a deadline, scope creeps endlessly. Push for a target date in this meeting.

⚠️ FGSPL Scope Ambiguity

BRD asks for UX consistency between GSLF and FGSPL websites. If FGSPL site isn't in scope, define this clearly in the contract.

⚠️ Chatbot Integration Dependency

Chatbot UI is yours; Zoho CRM + WhatsApp API is theirs. Without a clear integration owner on their side, you carry the blame when it doesn't work.

⚠️ Phase 2 Creep

Alumni network, blog, city SEO pages, research publications — the BRD lists these as Phase 2/3 but clients often ask for them at Phase 1 price.

⚠️ Multiple Stakeholder Sign-offs

CEO + COO + CTO all in the approval row. Ensure you have all three aligned before starting development — mid-project direction changes are expensive.

Our Suggestions — Value-Adds Beyond the BRD

What We'd Recommend They Consider

These are not in the BRD but are high-ROI additions worth raising. Present these as strategic recommendations — they show you're thinking like a product partner, not just a delivery vendor.

  • 1
    WhatsApp-first Student Journey — not just a sticky button 80%+ of GSLF's target students are on WhatsApp more than any website. Instead of just a sticky button, build a structured WhatsApp flow (via WhatsApp Business API) that mirrors the website journey — course enquiry, batch selection, Apply Now — all inside WhatsApp. This can capture 3–5× more leads than form fills alone, especially from Tier 2/3 cities.
  • 2
    Employer Trust Badges — not just logos The BRD asks for a "partner logo wall." We'd suggest upgrading this to quantified trust tiles: Voltas — 320 candidates hired | LG — 180 hired | Panasonic — 95 hired. A logo wall signals association. A hiring count signals proof. This is a direct conversion trigger for students and a strong credibility signal for new employers.
  • 3
    Live Batch Seat Counter — create urgency organically "Only 4 seats left — Pune batch starting July 20." This is a simple CMS-driven counter on each course page, updated by centre HODs. No engineering complexity. But it changes student behaviour from "I'll apply later" to "I need to apply now." Course pages with scarcity signals typically see 30–40% higher CTR on Apply Now.
  • 4
    A "For Parents" Section — India-specific conversion lever For GSLF's 18–24 age group, parents are often the real decision-makers. A dedicated section answering: "Is this safe? Will my child get a job? What is the salary? Is this government-certified?" — with NSDC/Skill India logos, placement data, and a parent enquiry WhatsApp number — directly addresses the person who often blocks enrollment. No competitor does this.
  • 5
    Google for Jobs + Course Schema Markup — free SEO leverage Marking up course pages with Google's Course and JobPosting structured data gets GSLF's courses surfaced directly in Google Search and Google for Jobs — without any ad spend. Given GSLF's trade-specific courses (EV Technician, RACW, Optical Fiber), these searches have high commercial intent. This is a one-time SEO setup that delivers compounding organic traffic.
  • 6
    Reels-ready Success Story Format — content that markets itself The BRD calls for 5 success stories. We'd suggest designing each story in a 9:16 vertical card format that GSLF can screenshot and post directly to Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts — zero extra design work. A student saying "I earn ₹22,000/month now — I trained at GSLF Pune" is the most powerful ad GSLF can run, and it costs nothing to distribute.
  • 7
    State Government MoU Showcase — underused credibility asset GSLF has active MoUs with multiple state governments. Most NGO websites bury this in About Us text. We'd recommend a dedicated visual strip on the homepage — state seals + MoU year — similar to how ed-tech companies show university partnerships. For rural students and parents, a state government endorsement is more credible than any corporate logo.
  • 8
    Monthly Impact Email — build a donor retention channel from Day 1 The BRD mentions a newsletter but doesn't define its purpose. We'd suggest positioning it specifically as a monthly Impact Update for donors and CSR partners — "This month: 142 students placed, 3 new state centres opened, spotlight: Priya from Nashik." Sent via Brevo/Mailchimp, automated from the CMS. Donors who receive consistent impact updates give 2–3× more than those who don't.
